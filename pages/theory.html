A clinical system for functioning under pressure

Human first.Then performance.

Executive Frame™ is a clinical architecture for individuals, families, and organisations navigating sustained pressure. It integrates emotional regulation, executive function, and self-determination into one operating system — applied across clinical, performance, and organisational contexts.

01 — The Premise

Most failures under pressure are not effort problems.
They are sequence problems.

Capable people do not fail under pressure because they lack effort, knowledge, or resolve.

They fail because the system narrows.

Under sustained load, the human nervous system shifts from access to protection. Perception funnels. Working memory contracts. Thinking accelerates and confidence rises in inverse proportion to accuracy. Action precedes reflection. The person remains present. The capacity does not.

This is not a character flaw. It is biology operating exactly as designed.

The work is not to override it. The work is to know it, name it, and have a sequence ready when it arrives.

02 — The Pattern

Instability. Confusion. Powerlessness.

When load increases, the system narrows in a recognisable order. Activation rises. Threat detection sharpens. Attention contracts. Working memory thins. Thinking becomes faster — and more certain than it should be. Behaviour becomes more reactive.

Stage 01

Instability

The body becomes reactive. Heart rate climbs. Sleep degrades. Sensation amplifies.

The body can lose its steadiness.

Maps to Centre — restores stability.
Stage 02

Confusion

Thinking becomes unclear. Cognitive distortions rise. Interpretation hardens around threat.

The mind can lose its accuracy.

Maps to Clarify — restores accuracy.
Stage 03

Powerlessness

Action becomes restricted. Avoidance, control, withdrawal. Agency narrows to immediate protection.

Agency can narrow.

Maps to Choose — restores agency.

Inside the Tunnel, urgency feels real. Thoughts feel accurate. Reactions feel justified. Capacity is reduced — and the reduction is invisible to the person experiencing it. Most breakdowns under pressure are sequence problems, not effort problems.

03 — The C³ Loop™

Centre. Clarify. Choose.

Executive Frame™ does not begin by attempting to fix what is happening. It begins by changing how the person responds in real time. At the centre of the system is one architecture, run in strict order.

The order is not stylistic. It mirrors the neurobiological sequence in which the brain comes back online: regulate, then think, then act. Reverse the order and the system fails. Skip a stage and the system fails. The order is the method.

Stage 01 · Centre Stabilise the system enough to think. +

When pressure rises, cognition is not the first move. Regulation is.

Centring is the work of returning the body to a state in which thinking is available. Not calm. Not relaxed. Regulated enough to access the prefrontal cortex.

The work is somatic before it is cognitive: interoceptive awareness, breath regulation, vagal engagement, attention to ground and posture. The aim is precise — restore the conditions under which clear thinking becomes possible.

Without it, every move that follows is compromised.

Grounded in: Polyvagal theory · Huberman applied neurobiology · Linehan distress tolerance · Perry neurosequential model
Stage 02 · Clarify Restore cognitive accuracy. +

Once the system stabilises, thinking becomes available — but it remains biased toward speed and certainty. Under residual pressure, the mind defaults to interpretation, not observation.

Clarifying is the work of distinguishing fact from story, signal from noise, urgency from priority. It is not more thinking. It is more accurate thinking.

The work is structural: externalise the variables, name what is known and what is assumed, separate what is being asked from what is being feared. Clarity does not require certainty. It requires sufficient perspective to see what is real.

Grounded in: Beck (CBT) · Barkley (executive function) · Hayes (ACT) · McGonigal (stress reappraisal)
Stage 03 · Choose Act with intention. +

From regulation and clarity, action becomes available — and deliberate.

Choosing is not the reward for the first two moves. It is the point of them. One step. Aligned. Owned. Not perfect, not exhaustive, not certain — but consistent with the person you intend to be under pressure.

This is where capacity expands, because action taken from clarity produces a different signal — to the system, to the situation, and to the people who depend on you.

The loop closes on action. Then it begins again.

Grounded in: Ryan & Deci (Self-Determination Theory) · Gollwitzer (implementation intentions) · Hayes (values-based action)
04 — In Real Time

S.H.I.F.T.™
The acute deployment.

The C³ Loop is the operating architecture. S.H.I.F.T.™ is its acute deployment — a four-move protocol designed to run when activation has overtaken metacognitive access. It is engineered for the moment, not the reflection.

S

Stop.

Interrupt the automatic response. Do not speak. Do not send. Do not move. The pattern locks if you let it.

H

Hold.

Stabilise the system. One physiological sigh. Ground the body before you engage the mind.

F

Focus.

Strip the narrative. Return to what is observable. What is real, what is within control — the rest is noise.

T

Take Action.

One specific next step. The loop closes on action, not on insight.

S.H.I.F.T. is how you run Centre → Clarify → Choose under pressure. It is not separate from the system. It is the system, deployed in thirty seconds.

06 — Outcomes

Outcomes are observable. Not promised.

Thinking becomes more available.

Not because pressure has improved. Because the person is no longer being run by it. The pressure does not disappear. The relationship to it changes.

07 — Integrated Foundations

One framework, quietly informed by many.

Three sciences sit at the centre of the architecture. A wider body of clinical and applied work shapes how they translate into practice — integrated where each tradition meaningfully strengthens the system, not stacked as a literature review.

The cognitive and behavioural traditions provide the structural scaffolding for change. Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (Beck) offers replicated methods for restructuring distorted thinking. Dialectical Behaviour Therapy contributes the capacity to hold contradiction without collapse. Acceptance and Commitment Therapy brings movement toward what matters, even in difficult internal weather.

Beneath these therapies sits the architecture of attention itself. Executive function research, anchored in the work of Russell Barkley, makes visible what most performance frameworks ignore: the cognitive systems that organise thought into action, what they cost, and how they degrade under sustained load.

Where regulation depends on relationship to self, the work of Kristin Neff and Paul Gilbert establishes self-compassion as a regulatory tool, not a sentiment. Their research demonstrates that warmth toward oneself is not a softening of standards — it is a precondition for sustained, high-stakes performance.

Underpinning all of this, contemporary neuroscience grounds the architecture in how the brain processes emotion, attention, motivation, and load. And positive psychology contributes the long-arc view: strengths, flourishing, and the conditions under which capacity expands rather than depletes.

The result is a framework that does not borrow from these traditions selectively. It integrates what works — where it works — into one operating system.
