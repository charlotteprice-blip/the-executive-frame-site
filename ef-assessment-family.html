Centre. Clarify. Choose.

Forty-eight honest statements — a clear, kind read of the capacity underneath your family life, starting with your own.

Rate each statement 1–5: 1 = Never · 5 = Almost always. Answer for the last few weeks, not your best week. There are no right answers — only honest ones.

Centre
Is the system steady enough to think clearly?

1.In the hardest moments of family life, I can name what I'm feeling — specifically, not just 'losing it'.

2.I know our family's pressure points and the times of day they arrive.

3.I'm aware of how my own state affects the atmosphere of our home.

4.When the household erupts, I can find my own steadiness before I respond.

5.My children see me experience emotions — and see me manage them well. That's the real lesson.

6.When family plans fall apart, I can adapt without taking it out on anyone.

7.Our family life is built around what genuinely matters to us.

8.In demanding stretches, I keep sight of the bigger story we're building.

9.I've chosen good-enough parenting deliberately — and my children benefit from that choice.

10.Behind difficult behaviour, I can usually find what my child actually needs.

11.My children and partner feel understood by me, and it shows.

12.I can genuinely consider my child's perspective before I respond.

13.Difficult conversations happen in our home without becoming conflicts.

14.My children share real things with me — and I work to keep it that way.

15.More often than not, I set the emotional tone of our home.

Clarify
Is the thinking organised enough to execute well?

16.I can recognise when my own day is influencing how I respond to my family.

17.I know my strengths as a parent, and I know my vulnerable times of day.

18.I notice my less helpful patterns early, while I can still change course.

19.I catch more sharp words before they're spoken than I used to — and the trend is in the right direction.

20.I think consequences through before I set them, so I can stand behind them.

21.When my child needs my attention, they get the real thing.

22.I can hold the whole family picture — who needs what, what's coming — without losing myself in it.

23.Family logistics live in shared systems, not just in my head.

24.The complex, multi-step logistics of family life get reliably managed.

25.After hard moments, I recover and reconnect quickly — with myself and with them.

26.My frustration is something I manage, not something the family weathers.

27.When my child is overwhelmed, I can stay steady until they're through it.

28.The connecting moments — play, conversation, time together — happen regularly, not just the logistics.

29.I sustain the long, patient work of raising children with consistency.

30.The routine demands of family life get met without consuming all of me.

31.Our family intentions become real, small steps — and they happen.

32.I can tell what truly matters from what's merely loudest, and I act on the difference.

33.I plan ahead for the predictable pressure points — transitions, term ends, holidays.

Choose
Is ownership intact enough to act deliberately?

34.The way we raise our children is considered and chosen — it's ours.

35.Our family genuinely runs on our values, visible in ordinary days.

36.We make the significant family decisions deliberately, together.

37.I'm a capable parent, and most days I know it.

38.Whatever stage comes next, I trust us to work it out — we have before.

39.I'm a better parent than I was a year ago, in ways I could describe.

40.I feel genuinely connected to my family — not just responsible for it.

41.I'm respected and appreciated in my home, including by my children in their own way.

42.Our home has real belonging in it — including mine.

43.Family life carries deep meaning for me, even amid the demands.

44.Watching my children grow — and growing alongside them — still moves me.

45.Parenting feels purposeful, even on the days it's hardest.

46.The demands of family life run me more than I run them.

47.I judge my parenting harshly more often than fairly.

48.I can feel alone even with my family around me.

Human first. Then performance.

Built for parents and caregivers: good-enough is the standard here, and honesty is the method. This is not a parenting-capacity or custody instrument. This is an educational self-reflection — quietly grounded in Goleman's emotional-intelligence research (Centre), Barkley's model of executive functioning (Clarify), and Deci & Ryan's Self-Determination Theory (Choose) — not a diagnostic instrument or a substitute for clinical assessment. The final three statements are scored in reverse. If anything here raises concern, your GP or a registered psychologist is a good first step — in a crisis dial 111 (NZ) / 000 (AU).
