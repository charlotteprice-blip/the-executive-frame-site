Centre. Clarify. Choose.

Forty-eight honest statements — a clear, professional read of the human capacity underneath your practice.

Rate each statement 1–5: 1 = Never · 5 = Almost always. Answer for the last few weeks, not your best week. There are no right answers — only honest ones.

Centre
Is the system steady enough to think clearly?

1.I'm aware of what demanding work is doing to me, while it's happening — not just afterwards.

2.I know which professional situations test me most, and I can see them coming.

3.I have an accurate sense of how I come across to clients and colleagues under pressure.

4.In high-stakes moments — hearings, negotiations, difficult calls — I can keep myself steady.

5.I can absorb the intensity of practice without passing it on to the people around me.

6.When a matter changes direction unexpectedly, I adapt with composure.

7.The work still connects to something that matters to me.

8.A poor outcome affects me without defining my week.

9.I deliver excellent work without polishing it past the point of value.

10.I can hear what a client actually needs beneath what they're asking for.

11.Colleagues and juniors raise problems with me early, because I respond to them well.

12.I can advocate firmly for one position while genuinely understanding the other.

13.I can give clients honest advice they may not want — and preserve their trust.

14.I'm regarded as both a strong advocate and a constructive professional to deal with.

15.I achieve more through judgement and presence than through force.

Clarify
Is the thinking organised enough to execute well?

16.I can tell when my advice is being shaped by the matter — and when it's being shaped by my own state.

17.I know when I do my best thinking, and I schedule my most demanding work accordingly.

18.I notice when I'm avoiding a piece of work, and I can address the reason rather than the symptom.

19.I give considered responses under provocation — the measured reply, not the immediate one.

20.Before acting on a matter, I think through the likely responses and consequences.

21.When I need deep focus on complex work, I can protect it from interruption.

22.I can hold the full architecture of a matter in mind while attending to its finest details.

23.Every commitment and undertaking I make is reliably recorded and tracked.

24.Procedural steps and court timetables are met in order, without last-minute crisis.

25.I recover my full capacity quickly after a demanding hearing or a difficult day.

26.The quality of my work is consistent, whatever I happen to be feeling.

27.Demanding clients receive my best professional self, not what's left over.

28.I tackle the most challenging work when I'm freshest, rather than deferring it.

29.My effort on long-running matters stays consistent from beginning to end.

30.The disciplines of practice — file notes, time records, schedules — are accurate and current.

31.My significant matters have clear, staged plans that a colleague could follow.

32.When everything is urgent, I can still order my work by what is genuinely important.

33.I anticipate complications early enough to prepare for them properly.

Choose
Is ownership intact enough to act deliberately?

34.The shape of my practice reflects choices I have made.

35.I can practise law in a way that is consistent with my values.

36.The significant decisions in my career have been my own.

37.I know I'm a capable lawyer, and the evidence supports it.

38.A novel legal problem engages my curiosity before my anxiety.

39.My professional skills are still developing, and I can see the growth.

40.My career and my closest relationships are both intact — and I intend to keep it that way.

41.My judgement is respected by the people I work with.

42.I belong in this profession.

43.Parts of this work are genuinely meaningful to me, beyond their commercial value.

44.The intellectual craft of the law still interests me.

45.My work serves a purpose I'm proud to describe.

46.The demands of practice control my time more than I do.

47.I privately doubt my ability more than my record justifies.

48.My work has come at a real cost to my relationships.

Human first. Then performance.

Built for legal practitioners: a private development tool, entirely separate from any question of fitness to practise. This is an educational self-reflection — quietly grounded in Goleman's emotional-intelligence research (Centre), Barkley's model of executive functioning (Clarify), and Deci & Ryan's Self-Determination Theory (Choose) — not a diagnostic instrument or a substitute for clinical assessment. The final three statements are scored in reverse. If anything here raises concern, your GP or a registered psychologist is a good first step — in a crisis dial 111 (NZ) / 000 (AU).
